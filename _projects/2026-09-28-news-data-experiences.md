Investigative and data journalism continue to serve the public good, but exist in an online attention environment governed by algorithms. Audiences, readers and communities are exposed to issues and learn in increasingly fractured ways, both online and offline. In-person data experiences offer new venues to meet readers where they are, bringing people together around data with playful in-real-life activities that spark curiosity and interest in published stories. This work explores techniques and principles that inform the idea of creating hands-on data experiences that translate data journalism into forms that attract diverse audiences, rebuild trust through face-to-face interaction, and reinforce stories with existing readers.
